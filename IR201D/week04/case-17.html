Case 17
IR 201 · Week 4 · Allison (2017)

Realist answer

Geography and other threats. An ocean away, America could not invade Britain, and Germany sat across the North Sea and was building a navy to rival Britain’s.

Liberal answer

Two democracies by the standards of the time, with deep trade and money ties. Each side expected the other to settle disputes by negotiation.

Constructivist answer

Identity. British leaders came to see Americans as kin, a friend who was rising, not an enemy.

Show of hands: which one explains the difference best? What would you need to see to be sure?

Allison’s five lessons from the Cold War, in short

  1. War between nuclear powers is suicideBoth sides know it.
  2. Still, each must be ready to risk a war it cannot winOtherwise the other side can win any standoff.
  3. Agree on rules of the roadArms control, rules at sea and in the air, limits on cyberattacks.
  4. What happens at home decidesThe Soviet economy fell behind. Which system will do better now?
  5. Have a strategy, not just hopeKennan’s containment took four years to build.

Data

  • GDP: Maddison Project Database 2023 (Bolt, Jutta, and Jan Luiten van Zanden. 2024. "Maddison style estimates of the evolution of the world economy: A new 2023 update." Journal of Economic Surveys), rug.nl/ggdc. GDP per person in 2011 international dollars times population, 2022 is the last year. Countries within the database’s borders. The Soviet series covers the former USSR and is used for Russia too. Before 1820 the database has only a few benchmark years, so the early cases have no chart.
  • Capability index: Correlates of War, National Material Capabilities v7.0, 1816 to 2022 (Singer, Bremer, and Stuckey 1972), correlatesofwar.org. CINC combines six counts with equal weight: military spending, military personnel, iron and steel production, energy use, urban population, total population. Germany is Prussia before 1871.
  • Seesaw: after Graham Allison’s slide "Who’s rebalancing whom?" in his TED talk "Is war between China and the US inevitable?" (2018). His figures (IMF, Economist Intelligence Unit) put China at 47%, 105% and, projected, 142% of the US in 2004, 2014 and 2024. Ours are World Bank GDP at purchasing power parity in current international dollars. Price bases differ, so compare the percentages, not the dollar amounts.
  • US and China since 1980: World Bank, World Development Indicators, GDP at market exchange rates and at purchasing power parity. SIPRI Military Expenditure Database (v1.2). Nuclear warheads: Federation of American Scientists via Our World in Data. All as used on the Week 2 page "One superpower?".

Notes

  • Where Allison’s ruling side is several states, their numbers are added together. In case 13 that makes Germany small next to the Soviet Union, France and Britain combined.
  • Allison speaks of a rising power that threatens to displace a ruling one, often in one domain such as the seas. A rising power can be far smaller than the ruling one in total, as Japan was in 1941.
  • CINC rewards population, steel and energy. That is why it puts China ahead of the US in the 1990s, when China’s economy was still much smaller.
