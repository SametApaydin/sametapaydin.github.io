Your call

Unipolar, bipolar, multipolar, or multiplex? Which measure decides it for you?

Brooks and Wohlforth weigh military reach, technology, profits and alliances, and call today's world one of "partial unipolarity". Others, such as Øystein Tunsjø (2018), see a US-China two-power world: China is closing the gap with the US and is far ahead of any third state. Economic size, manufacturing and exports point the same way. On nuclear weapons the world still looks like the Cold War pair. Amitav Acharya (2017) asks whether counting poles is the right question at all. Each answer depends on which measure you think decides how international politics works.
